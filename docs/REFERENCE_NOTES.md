# Research and design references

AstroGuard keeps its existing dark mission-control dashboard treatment and uses the reference material to make the workflow clearer. The app does not copy agency branding, logos, photography, or claim an agency affiliation. The new radiation illustration is CSS-generated; no external photos are bundled.

## References consulted

- [NASA: Wearable Tech for Space Station Research](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/wearable-tech-for-space-station-research/) — informs the separation of wearable health observations from dosimeter exposure records.
- [Canadian Space Agency: The TBone studies](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/sciences/tbone.asp) — informs a dedicated bone-health record for imaging and other direct source measurements, rather than estimating bone changes from routine vital signs.
- [Wikimedia Commons: Akihiko Hoshide using ultrasound](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ISS-65_Astronaut_Akihiko_Hoshide_scans_his_leg_with_an_ultrasound_device.jpg) — reviewed as an example of astronaut health research imagery. The public-domain photograph is not embedded in this application.

These pages are design and domain context only. They are not connected data sources for AstroGuard. All app demonstration measurements remain synthetic and locally generated.
